PREDICTION — written before the judge prompt was iterated
=========================================================
Written: 2026-09-22 (before agreement_after was measured; see the sealed receipt in
evaluation/week6/labels_receipt.json for the ordering of the label file, and
course/results/week6_agreement.json for the run timestamps.)

One sentence:

  Adding two of the judge's own disagreements as few-shot examples will fix its tendency to
  FAIL answers that are correct but terse - answers that give the right figure without
  restating every condition - and agreement will rise mainly on the incomplete_conditions
  and citation_traceability modes, with no change on correct_abstention.

Why I expect that:

  The v1 prompt tells the judge to fail an answer when "a material condition is missing".
  A small judge model reads that strictly and treats brevity as incompleteness, so I expect
  its errors to be concentrated in false FAILs on short-but-correct answers rather than
  false PASSes. Showing it two real cases where a short answer was still complete should
  recalibrate that boundary.

What would make this prediction wrong:

  If the disagreements turn out to be mostly false PASSes (the judge accepting answers a
  human failed), then my model of the failure is backwards, and few-shot examples of the
  opposite kind would be needed. It would also be wrong if agreement moved on
  correct_abstention, which I expect to be already at ceiling because refusal is an exact
  string match.
